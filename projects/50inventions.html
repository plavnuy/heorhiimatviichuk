Overview

The project was created for the Presidential Administration of Ukraine as part of the exhibition “50 Inventions Bestowed by Ukraine to the World”, highlighting Ukrainian scientific, engineering, and cultural achievements that had a global impact.

I designed four posters as part of the creative team Direct69, with Alexander Ivanyshyn acting as the art director of the project. The visual language focused on clarity, monumentality, and historical weight, translating complex technological achievements into bold, readable graphic statements.

Two of the posters — “ZEPPELINS RETURN BACK TO THE SKIES” and “Antonov-225 Mriya” — were exhibited in the main hall of the Presidential Administration of Ukraine, becoming part of the official exhibition space.

ZEPPELINS RETURN BACK TO THE SKIES

After the tragic crash of the Hindenburg zeppelin in 1937, it was believed that the era of airships had ended. However, aircraft designs by Igor Pasternak (born in 1964 in Lviv) brought zeppelins back into the skies. His company, Worldwide Aeros Corp, developed the Aeroscraft zeppelin with a payload capacity of up to 250 tons, capable of operating with significantly lower fuel consumption and landing without airfields.

ANTONOV-225 — WORLD’S LARGEST AIRCRAFT

Antonov-225 Mriya remains the largest and heaviest aircraft ever built. Designed in Kyiv, it set over 100 world records and became a symbol of Ukrainian engineering excellence and ambition.

NUCLEAR ICEBREAKERS AND SUBMARINES

Academician Anatoly Oleksandrov, born in Ukraine, played a key role in the development of the Soviet nuclear fleet. Under his leadership, nuclear power plants were created for submarines and icebreakers, enabling year-round navigation in Arctic regions.

EKRANOPLAN: THE CASPIAN SEA MONSTER

The Caspian Sea Monster ekranoplan, designed by Rostyslav Alekseyev, was a groundbreaking ground-effect vehicle that combined principles of aviation and marine engineering. For many years it held the record as the world’s heaviest aircraft before being surpassed by the Antonov-225 Mriya.

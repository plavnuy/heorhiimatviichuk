Role & Partnership

The partnership began in 2025 when founder Bogdan approached me to collaborate on developing a logo symbol for the project. Since then, I've served as a design partner across multiple dimensions of the project.

My involvement spans strategic consultation, creative brainstorming, material design, and developing art direction solutions. This ongoing collaboration represents a symbiotic relationship between conceptual thinking and visual execution.

Logo

The logo development began with research into historical Ukrainian typography and graphic traditions, aiming to preserve cultural roots while establishing a contemporary visual language.

During archival research, a stylized letter "K" was discovered whose form naturally suggested the numerals "1" and "9." This discovery formed the conceptual foundation—merging letter and numbers into a unified graphic symbol that represents both name and number.


The resulting mark functions as both wordmark and symbolic identifier, adaptable across various applications from digital interfaces to physical exhibition materials.

Transformation. Dase

Roma Dase’s first solo exhibition, presented at MLYN Design Hub, marked a significant transition from tattoo culture into contemporary art practice. The project explored abstraction, form and color as primary visual languages, translating the artist’s internal transformation into a spatial and graphic narrative.

My role in the exhibition focused on shaping its visual and informational layer. I developed the core informational poster describing the exhibition concept, designed the main poster and event video, and created the printed exhibition catalog. These materials functioned not as standalone artifacts, but as a cohesive system supporting the curatorial idea and guiding the viewer through the exhibition’s themes.

In addition to graphic and motion design, I assisted with the physical installation of the exhibition and participated in the composition and arrangement of the canvases within the space. This hands-on involvement allowed the visual identity to remain consistent across digital, printed and spatial elements, reinforcing the exhibition’s sense of transition and emergence.

“Transformation” functioned as both theme and process—documenting the artist’s journey through transition, exploration and the materialization of internal visions into tangible artistic expressions. The exhibition design created deliberate moments of discovery, allowing visitors to experience the threshold between artistic disciplines and witness the formation of a new creative voice.

“LIMINAL” Exhibition

Ukrainian Design and Innovation Week (UDIW)


“Liminal” explores the state of transition — the threshold between the known and the unknown — where design functions not as a finished product, but as a process of becoming. The exhibition positions design as an instrument of transformation, discovery and the emergence of new forms, meanings and practices.

Focusing on the principles of design thinking, the exhibition reveals what is usually concealed from public view: sketches, prototypes, materials and intermediate production stages. Objects are presented in their liminal state — unfinished, exploratory and open-ended — allowing visitors to encounter design at the moment where ideas take shape and decisions are still in flux.


The spatial and graphic identity of the exhibition reinforced this conceptual framework. As part of the art direction and graphic design, I developed the main exhibition poster and entrance signage, and contributed to the overall visual coherence of the space. A key conceptual decision was the use of yellow adhesive tape to mark and unify furniture and object zones. The tape functioned as a visual metaphor for the design process itself — provisional, process-driven and intentionally visible — while the color yellow symbolized emergence, initiation and the beginning of form.

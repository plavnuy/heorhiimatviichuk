ShareSpot

Type Startup
Focus Peer-to-Peer Parking Exchange
Timeline 27 days
Role Product & UI/UX Designer

Overview

ShareSpot is a mobile-first platform that connects drivers looking for parking with those ready to exchange their spots at scheduled times.

The product addresses one of the most stressful urban mobility problems: finding a legal parking spot quickly in densely packed areas.

The design focuses on decision-making under pressure, minimal cognitive load while driving, and trust-building between two sides of the marketplace.

Understanding the Problem

Drivers in dense urban areas waste significant time and attention searching for parking, often under stress, time pressure, and risk of fines or towing.

At the same time, many parked drivers are willing to monetize their spot but lack a simple, reliable way to connect with incoming drivers.

Discovery Activities

  • User interviews with urban drivers.
  • Lean Canvas to define the marketplace structure.
  • JTBD statements to capture real-world motivations.
  • Analysis of situational and emotional drivers.

Design Process

The project followed a structured but lean process, balancing speed with depth of exploration.

Timeline:
Discovery (3 days) → Definition (8 days) → Design (16 days)

Each phase focused on reducing uncertainty around driver behavior, interaction risks, and real-world constraints.

Jobs To Be Done

JTBD statements were used to ground the product in real contexts rather than idealized user flows.

Primary Job:
When I’m already in or approaching a densely packed area and short on time, I want to secure a legal parking spot quickly, so I can stop wasting attention, avoid fines, and focus on my destination.

For spot owners, the job is reversed: monetizing a parking spot without adding friction or uncertainty to their own schedule.

Key Usage Contexts

Searching Driver

  • Driving or en route in a dense urban area.
  • High stress, limited attention, time pressure.
  • Decision criteria: speed, simplicity, legal certainty.
  • Interface must support one-handed use and clear readability.

Spot Owner

  • Already parked and preparing to leave.
  • Motivated by compensation for time and effort.
  • Needs fast setup and reliable reminders.
  • Values flexibility in defining departure time.

Driver Interaction Map

A detailed interaction map was created to account for real-world failures, including late arrivals, cancellations, and no-shows.

The system introduces compensation rules, reminders, and fallback scenarios to reduce conflict and protect both sides of the exchange.

The goal was not to eliminate failure, but to design predictable outcomes when failure occurs.

Information Architecture

The information architecture prioritizes the most critical actions over secondary settings and profile management.

The structure is intentionally minimal for driving contexts, while supporting deeper planning and history views when the user is stationary.

This adaptive approach reduces cognitive load and aligns interface complexity with the user’s situational capacity.

UI Design & Prototype

The UI library was designed to support fast interaction, high contrast, and clear hierarchy under real-world conditions.

An interactive prototype was built to validate flows, timing, and error states before moving into development discussions.

Visual design decisions were driven by usability under stress rather than aesthetic minimalism alone.
